    You lay the crown on the marble, and the three shards beside it, and call her name.

    Isolde comes, as she always would have, if anyone had asked. Her wedding gown is dry. She kneels, and Oswin kneels, and between them lies the crown, and the two of them look at each other for the first time in a hundred years.

    "I tried to take it off," he says. "I tried so hard."

    "I know," she says. "I know."

    Together, they speak the crown's true name. The iron unmakes itself, quietly, into a handful of warm dust. The water leaves the fen in a single long breath, and the sun comes up over a green and ordinary country.

    You are alone in a very empty hall. On the throne, someone has left a bell, whole and bright, and a note, in a queen's careful hand: *Thank you.*
